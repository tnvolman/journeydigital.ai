## The honest answer: it depends — but here's what actually drives the price.

If you've started searching for a church website, you've probably seen quotes ranging from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands. That range is real, and it's confusing. Let's break it down.

## What you're usually paying for

### DIY / template builders ($0–$50/month)

Squarespace, Wix, and similar tools can get a basic site up fast. You're doing all the work yourself, the design is template-bound, and the result often looks like every other church on the internet. There's no shame in starting here — but there's a ceiling.

### Custom church studio — Journey's lane (from $2,000)

This is where Journey Digital lives. A small studio builds you a complete, custom church website — design, development, mobile, sermon archives, forms, maps, and launch — without the account-manager tax of a big agency. We use AI-assisted workflows so the work stays careful and the price stays honest. Not a template with your logo dropped in. Yours.

### Full-service agencies ($5,000–$20,000+)

Full-service digital agencies bring teams, process, and polish. You're also paying for overhead: account managers, project coordinators, and office space. For many churches, that overhead doesn't translate to a better website — it just translates to a bigger invoice.

## What Journey Digital charges — and why

We price from **$2,000** for a complete, custom church website. That's not a template fee with add-ons bolted on. It's the whole thing: design, development, mobile optimization, sermon archives, contact forms, Google Maps integration, and launch.

## What drives the price up

- **Online giving integration** — third-party processors (Tithe.ly, Pushpay, Stripe) each have their own setup requirements
- **Custom member portals** — if you need login-gated content, that's a separate build
- **Large media archives** — hundreds of sermons require thoughtful architecture
- **Ongoing retainers** — if you want us managing updates and content month-to-month, we can talk

## What to ask any web vendor

1. Can I see church websites you've built?
2. Who owns the site when it's done?
3. What happens if I need changes six months from now?
4. Is hosting included or separate?
5. What does the handoff look like?

A good vendor answers all five without hesitation.
